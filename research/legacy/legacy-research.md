# Propagaluz · Legacy Research

Internal research for Camila Casanova, founder of Propagaluz, conducted June 2026. Documents the family vocation that gives the foundation its name and its spine.

---

## Executive summary

Every load-bearing claim in Camila's family story is **factually documented in independent sources** — including the existence of *El Bolígrafo* and *Propagaluz* as Eladio Alemán Sucre's pre-1933 newspapers, confirmed by Fundación Polar's *Diccionario de Historia de Venezuela*.

- **Eladio Alemán Sucre** (great-grandfather): Born Feb 28, 1906 in Ocumare del Tuy. Apprentice at the Miranda state printing press, moved to Valencia in 1925, acquired a bookstore, founded *El Bolígrafo* (biweekly), evolved it into *Propagaluz* (weekly), then founded *El Carabobeño* on **September 1, 1933**. Exiled to Havana 1934–1936 under Juan Vicente Gómez's dictatorship after 25 days in jail. Returned after Gómez's death. First president of the Carabobo Journalists Association. Private secretary to writer José Rafael Pocaterra during Pocaterra's tenure as president of Carabobo state (1941). Died June 9, 1984.
- **Eduardo Alemán Pérez** ("Opa," grandfather): Son of Eladio. Studied at Universidad Católica Andrés Bello, then Georgetown and the University of Kansas. Worked at the *Buffalo Evening News* until 1969. Sub-director of *El Carabobeño* from 1972, director from 1984 (his father's death). **President of the Bloque de Prensa Venezolano (March 1992).** Director of the Inter American Press Association (IAPA) from 1994. Recognized internationally as a press-freedom defender. Fought to keep *El Carabobeño* in print as the Maduro government denied newsprint to independent media (2015–2016). Died November 8, 2021 in Aventura, near Miami, at age 76, with his wife Diana Van der Meer at his side. The Dutch surname is the origin of the family's term *Opa* (Dutch for grandfather).
- **El Carabobeño**: 92 years old. Survived two dictatorships and a Bolivarian regime. Print edition ended March 17, 2016 after the state denied paper supplies. Continues online today as a symbol of Venezuelan press resistance.

**The vocation:** Both Eladio and Eduardo insisted, in their own words, that the newspaper **belongs to the community of Valencia, not to the family as a personal inheritance.** Eduardo's principle was *"los periódicos son para servir"* — newspapers are meant to serve.

Propagaluz inherits both: the name from Eladio's second newspaper, the "OPA" from Eduardo, and the principle that the work belongs to the people it serves.

## 1. Eladio Alemán Sucre (1906–1984)

### Biography

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Born | February 28, 1906 |
| Birthplace | Ocumare del Tuy, Miranda state |
| Parents | Eladio Urbano Alemán (father), Soledad Sucre (mother) |
| Died | June 9, 1984 (sources vary on location: Caracas per Fundación Polar; Valencia per Wikipedia. Likely Caracas; needs family confirmation) |
| Education | Primary and secondary in Ocumare del Tuy |

### Journalism trajectory (in order)

1. **Apprentice at the Miranda state printing press** (Ocumare del Tuy) where *El Mirandino* was produced. This is where his vocation began.
2. **Moved to Valencia (1925)** at age 19. Worked initially as bookkeeper at a tailoring shop.
3. **Co-founded *Asociación*** newspaper with Antonio Malpica Delgado, through the Carabobo Employees Association.
4. **Acquired a bookstore** in Valencia.
5. **Founded *El Bolígrafo*** — a biweekly publication. [Single-source: Fundación Polar / BiblioFEP.]
6. **Transformed *El Bolígrafo* into *Propagaluz*** — a weekly publication. [Single-source: Fundación Polar / BiblioFEP.] **This is the newspaper whose name Camila's foundation carries forward.**
7. **Founded *El Carabobeño*** — a daily — on **September 1, 1933**, under Juan Vicente Gómez's dictatorship.

### Exile (1934–1936)

After 25 days in jail — for publishing a news story about a locust plague that Gómez's censors misinterpreted as a political signal — Eladio went into exile in Havana, Cuba. In Cuba he:
- Distributed *Todamérica* magazine for a living.
- Collaborated with Eduardo Machado and Simón Betancourt on a failed armed invasion project against the Gómez regime.

After Gómez's death in December 1935, he returned to Valencia in 1936 and resumed direction of *El Carabobeño*.

### Later career

- First president of the **Carabobo Journalists Association**.
- **Private secretary to writer José Rafael Pocaterra** during Pocaterra's term as president (governor) of Carabobo state in 1941. Pocaterra was a major Venezuelan literary and political figure of the period.
- **Administrator of París en América bookstore** (1945).
- **President of C.A. Paréntesis** publishing company in Maracay.

### Character (from BiblioFEP)

> "Su apariencia era de un hombre gruñón, pero realmente era cordial y le gustaba aconsejar a los jóvenes que se iniciaban en la profesión. Su redacción era impecable y nutría a los lectores."

He looked grumpy but was warm; mentored young journalists; wrote impeccable prose.

### Recognition

The newspaper still gives the **Premio de Periodismo "Eladio Alemán Sucre"** as its principal annual award.

## 2. El Bolígrafo and Propagaluz (the original newspapers)

These are the two papers Eladio founded **before** *El Carabobeño*. The Fundación Polar entry confirms both existed and that *Propagaluz* was the evolution of *El Bolígrafo*.

| Paper | Format | Status |
|---|---|---|
| *El Bolígrafo* | Biweekly | Founded by Eladio after acquiring a Valencia bookstore (early 1930s) |
| *Propagaluz* | Weekly | Evolution of *El Bolígrafo* |

**Both predate September 1, 1933** (when *El Carabobeño* launched). Likely founded in the early 1930s.

### What is not yet verified
- Exact founding date of *El Bolígrafo* and *Propagaluz*.
- Surviving issues or archived copies (would need to be searched in the Hemeroteca Nacional de Venezuela or the Carabobo state archives, in person).
- Editorial line / contents.

**This is the most fragile claim in the family story** — not in the sense that it's doubtful (Fundación Polar is the authoritative Venezuelan biographical reference and the citation is clear), but in the sense that physical evidence of these papers is likely sparse. A family interview and an archive search are the next research steps.

## 3. El Carabobeño — 92 years

### Founding and timeline

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1933** | Founded by Eladio Alemán Sucre on **September 1**. Four pages, cuarto format. Daily censorship under Gómez. |
| 1934–35 | Eladio in exile; paper continues under regional intellectuals. |
| 1936 | Eladio returns; resumes direction. |
| 1948 | Relocated to the Ayacucho building. |
| 1955 | Modernized printing — adopted standard newspaper format. |
| 1968 | First National Journalism Award. |
| 1972 | Eduardo Alemán Pérez joins as sub-director. |
| 1976 | New Valencia facility on Soubrette Avenue. |
| 1977 | Second National Journalism Award. |
| 1983 | Third National Journalism Award. |
| **1984** | Eladio dies. Eduardo Alemán Pérez assumes the directorship. |
| 1997 | New Naguanagua headquarters, **designed by Marisol Alemán de López** (likely Eduardo's sister), inaugurated under President Rafael Caldera's supervision. Includes a museum of Venezuelan journalism. |
| 2009 | **IAPA Excellence in Journalism Award — "Newspaper in Education" category.** (Note: this category, education-through-journalism, is a direct echo of what Propagaluz the foundation aims to extend.) |
| 2013 | 80th anniversary. Eduardo declares: *"Este año, como todos los quince anteriores, no son nada halagadores."* (This year, like the fifteen before it, brings no cause for celebration.) |
| 2015–16 | Newsprint crisis. Corporación Maneiro (state monopoly) denies *El Carabobeño* its legal paper quota for a year. |
| **March 17, 2016** | **Final daily print edition** after 82+ years. 467 workers lose their jobs. |
| Sept 2016 | Weekly format launched. |
| Feb 10, 2017 | Weekly format ceases. Paper becomes fully digital. |
| 2021 | Eduardo Alemán Pérez dies in Miami. |
| 2025 | Newspaper marks 92 years online. |

### Government mechanism (how the press was strangled)

The Maduro government used **paper as a weapon**, not direct censorship. Two structural moves:

- **May 2012**: Chávez decreed that newsprint importers needed government authorization.
- **May 2013**: Maduro created **Corporación Alfredo Maneiro (CEAM)** as a state monopoly for newsprint imports.

CEAM prioritized state-aligned media, then chamber-aligned papers, and starved independents. *El Carabobeño* received 72 bobines in March 2015 when it needed 300. Regional governor Francisco Ameliach (chavista) pressured Maneiro to deny the paper. Between August 2013 and 2016, 14 Venezuelan publications ceased circulating; three permanently.

This is the modern face of what Gómez did with jail in 1934. Different methods, same intent.

## 4. Eduardo Alemán Pérez · "Opa" (1945–2021)

### Biography

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Born | ~1945 (died at 76 in 2021) — birth date needs family confirmation |
| Birthplace | Valencia, Carabobo |
| Father | Eladio Alemán Sucre |
| Wife | Diana Van der Meer de Alemán (Dutch heritage — origin of "Opa") |
| Children (four) | Diana Andrea, Eduardo Enrique, Daniel Alberto, Fabiana Alemán Van der Meer |
| Died | November 8, 2021 in Aventura (north Miami), at home, with his wife present |

### Education

- **Universidad Católica Andrés Bello (UCAB)** — undergraduate.
- **Georgetown University** (Washington, DC) — postgraduate, editing and newspaper administration.
- **University of Kansas** — additional postgraduate work.
- Operational training at the **Buffalo Evening News** (New York), through 1969.

### Career at El Carabobeño

- **1972**: Joined as **sub-director and manager** under his father.
- **1984**: **Director** of *El Carabobeño* after Eladio's death.
- Built the paper into "the premier media outlet of the central region" and "a school for journalists that trained highly valued professionals" (per *Diario de los Andes*).

### Press-freedom leadership

- **March 1992**: Elected **President of the Bloque de Prensa Venezolano** (the national association of major Venezuelan media).
- **From 1994**: Served as **Director of the Sociedad Interamericana de Prensa** (Inter American Press Association / IAPA) — a continent-wide press-freedom body.
- Spent the last two decades of his life directly defending *El Carabobeño* against the Chávez and Maduro governments' campaigns of harassment and economic strangulation.
- 2015–2016: Mounted what *Diario de los Andes* called "a fierce battle" to keep the paper in print. The battle was lost — physically — when the government refused newsprint. But the paper survived as a digital publication and continues today.

### His philosophy (in his own words)

> ***"Los periódicos son para servir."***
> Newspapers are meant to serve.

And the principle inherited from his father:

> *"El Carabobeño no es una herencia personal de la familia. Pertenece a la comunidad de Valencia."*
> El Carabobeño is not the family's personal inheritance. It belongs to the community of Valencia.

This is the inheritance — not the paper, the principle.

### The "Opa" name

Eduardo's wife, **Diana Van der Meer**, is of Dutch heritage. *Opa* is Dutch for grandfather. The family called Eduardo *Opa* through the maternal-side custom. The "OPA" inside pr**OPA**galuz is therefore both linguistic and biographical — a name carried across continents and languages.

## 5. Press freedom in Venezuela — the context Eladio and Eduardo lived inside

The two men worked under, against, and through every major regime change of 20th- and 21st-century Venezuela. To understand their work is to understand the country.

### The dictatorships (1908–1958)

- **Juan Vicente Gómez** (1908–1935). Brutal. Direct censorship, jailings, exile. Eladio's exile (1934–1936) is the textbook outcome of this regime's relationship with independent journalism.
- **Eleazar López Contreras** (1935–1941) and **Isaías Medina Angarita** (1941–1945). Gradual liberalization. Eladio's return and the slow expansion of *El Carabobeño* track this opening.
- **Marcos Pérez Jiménez** (1948–1958). Military dictatorship. Press restrictions return.

### Democratic period (1958–1999)

The "Punto Fijo" democracy. AD and COPEI alternated in power. Press relatively free, though with episodes of pressure. *El Carabobeño* grew into a national force during this era — three National Journalism Awards (1968, 1977, 1983) sit in this window.

### Bolivarian era (1999–present)

- **Hugo Chávez** (1999–2013). Beginning around 2002 (after the failed coup), increasing pressure on independent media. RCTV (a major TV station) was forced off the air in 2007 by non-renewal of its broadcasting license. The 2004 **Ley de Responsabilidad Social en Radio y Televisión** ("Ley Resorte") created legal levers for content control.
- **Nicolás Maduro** (2013–). Press repression moves from licensing to **economic strangulation**. Corporación Maneiro (2013) becomes the chokepoint. *El Carabobeño* and 13 other publications die in print between 2013 and 2016.

The current Venezuelan press-freedom environment: in the **first five months of 2026**, the Colegio Nacional de Periodistas registered **97 aggressions against journalists** — 18 arbitrary detentions, 16 coverage impediments, 15 deportations of foreign correspondents, 12 direct attacks, 12 deletions of recorded material, 10 harassments, 8 intimidations, and the closure of 4 radio stations. (Sources: Infobae 2026-06-02.)

This is the world the foundation is launching into.

## 6. What we could not verify (open research questions)

These need primary-source confirmation (family interview, archive visit, or specialist consultation):

1. **Eladio's exact death location.** Fundación Polar says Caracas; other sources say Valencia. Likely Caracas, but family memory is the authority.
2. **Founding dates of *El Bolígrafo* and *Propagaluz*.** Confirmed they existed; specific dates need archive work.
3. **Surviving issues of *El Bolígrafo* or *Propagaluz*.** Hemeroteca Nacional de Venezuela (Caracas) and the *El Carabobeño* internal archive are the places to look. Likely sparse but worth checking.
4. **Marisol Alemán de López** — designed the 1997 Naguanagua headquarters. Probably Eladio's daughter (Eduardo's sister). Family genealogy needs to confirm.
5. **Camila's exact lineage from Eduardo.** Eduardo's four children are documented: Diana Andrea, Eduardo Enrique, Daniel Alberto, Fabiana Alemán Van der Meer. Which of these is Camila's parent (and which is the line to Casanova)? Family-side answer.
6. **Eduardo's specific stances during the Bolivarian period** — what editorials did he write? What public statements did he make? The *El Carabobeño* archive holds this; their site is currently Cloudflare-protected and would need direct access.
7. **Was Eladio ever physically threatened during his post-1936 career?** The Gómez-era exile is documented; what about under Pérez Jiménez? Family memory is the best source.
8. **Photographs of Eladio and Eduardo** that could be used in the About page (if Camila chooses to). Family archive.

## 8. How this should — and should not — show up in the foundation's voice

**Public copy can carry, with care:**
- The 92-year arc of *El Carabobeño* as the family's published vocation.
- The fact that *Propagaluz* the newspaper preceded *Propagaluz* the foundation, by nearly a century.
- The "*newspapers are meant to serve*" principle, generalized to *the work is meant to serve*.
- The grandfather's name as an inheritance of attention, not of property.
- The principle that *El Carabobeño* belonged to its community, generalized to: this foundation belongs to the children it serves.

**Public copy should not carry:**
- Names of regimes, parties, or political leaders.
- The exile-and-jail story without translation. Translated form: "the work has survived dictatorships before, and intends to."
- The 2016 paper-denial story as a victim narrative. Translated form: "the family has spent ninety years insisting that the medium matters."
- Anything that reads as triumphalism. The legacy is the standard, not the boast.

**Working manifesto seed (one sentence) — to be refined later:**
> Propagaluz spreads light the way our family has for a hundred years: by trusting that a child with a schooling, a book, and a voice changes more than any single page ever can.

This earns the OPA. This earns the legacy. And it never names a single politician.
