# Partner ecosystem — research and recommendation

Where the donations should go, and why. Researched June 2026. The conclusion is in §5.

---

## 1. The decision frame

Propagaluz's funding model is a **conduit**: gather donations outside Venezuela, channel them to vetted partner organizations doing direct educational work on the ground. The questions are:
- **Which partner(s)?**
- **One, or several?**
- **What about working directly with schools?**

The two non-negotiables from `BRAND.md` and `PRODUCT.md`:
- Education for underserved Venezuelan children (3–20) is the primary cause.
- Freedom of speech / the right to be heard is the second cause, framed as the *outcome* of education.

Any partner choice has to answer both.

## 2. Fe y Alegría — the obvious primary

### What they are
A Jesuit-founded movement of popular education and social promotion. Founded **March 5, 1955** by Father José María Vélaz in the 23 de Enero parish of Caracas, with help from students at the **Universidad Católica Andrés Bello** — the same university Eduardo Alemán Pérez (Opa) attended. They now operate **200+ educational centers in Venezuela** and have expanded to 22+ countries across Latin America, Africa, and Asia. 70 years old.

### Why they fit propagaluz
- **Operational maturity.** Seven decades of running schools in the exact populations propagaluz wants to serve.
- **Scale.** Propagaluz's first-year donations, however modest, drop into a system that already moves children through schools daily. Money lands somewhere real.
- **Spanish chapter handles legal/tax in Madrid.** Entreculturas (their Spanish affiliate, in Madrid at Calle Maldonado 1) is the funnel. Donations made in Spain to Entreculturas are tax-deductible. **This solves the Spain→Venezuela money-movement problem cleanly.**
- **Fundación Lealtad accreditation** — independent transparency seal that propagaluz can cite.
- **The UCAB / Opa connection.** Fe y Alegría was founded by UCAB students. Eduardo studied at UCAB. The institutional thread is real, not invented.
- **Their 2020 Infobae profile**: "the key organization in containment and education of the poorest in the midst of Venezuela's humanitarian crisis." This is the bar.

### The Jesuit / Catholic question (honestly addressed)
Camila asked: does the Jesuit framing close doors?

**Honest read: not meaningfully, not for propagaluz's actual donor base.**

- **Spanish, Venezuelan, and Latin American donors** are overwhelmingly Catholic-cultured — even when individually secular. The Jesuit name is *recognizable and respected*, not alien.
- **Jesuit education has a particular reputation**: serious, intellectually rigorous, socially engaged. The "Christian values" framing in Fe y Alegría's mission is sincere but not aggressive — it does not exclude non-Catholic students or staff in practice.
- **Anti-clerical filtering**: a small slice of secular Venezuelan progressives may prefer not to fund a Catholic org. This is real but small.
- **International / institutional donors (US foundations, EU agencies, UN partners)** treat Fe y Alegría as a gold-standard local partner. It opens doors, doesn't close them.
- **Venezuelan press-freedom context**: the Catholic Church has historically been one of the more consistent voices critical of the Chávez/Maduro regimes. Aligning with a Catholic education org is *not* in tension with the foundation's free-speech values; if anything it is in lineage with them.

**Verdict: Jesuit framing is an asset for v1, not a filter.** Recommend Fe y Alegría as primary.

### Risks
- **Brand dilution.** Propagaluz may read as "small donations forwarded to Fe y Alegría" unless propagaluz's own story is told well. Cure: the About page and manifesto must establish propagaluz's unique position (trust layer, diaspora bridge, family vocation).
- **Single-partner concentration.** If Fe y Alegría hits trouble (institutional or political), propagaluz's giving model is exposed. Cure: add a second partner.

## 3. CECODAP — the recommended secondary

### What they are
**Centros Comunitarios de Aprendizaje**, founded **1984** in Caracas. Began as community learning centers, evolved into Venezuela's leading children's-rights advocacy organization. **Secular.** Forty-plus years of work. Has a publishing arm called *Ediciones El Papagayo*. Over 1,200 national and international media appearances. Recognized partner of Amnistía Internacional Venezuela.

### Why they fit
- **Secular** — balances Fe y Alegría's religious framing.
- **Their explicit method is participation by children, adolescents, and families.** Children are not just objects of help — they are participants in the construction of "coexistence without violence." **This is the "voice" pillar in operational form.**
- **They publish educational materials.** Propagaluz could fund a specific publication, a teacher-training module, or a series of children-authored pieces.
- **Smaller scale than Fe y Alegría** — propagaluz's donations land with proportionally more impact, more visibility, more directness.
- **Different funding texture.** Fe y Alegría = bricks-and-mortar schools. CECODAP = advocacy, training, voice. Two complementary shapes of help.

### Risks
- **No Spanish chapter equivalent to Entreculturas.** Funds to CECODAP would go directly from Spain to Venezuela — more legal/banking complexity.
- **Smaller organization** = higher per-dollar oversight burden for propagaluz.
- **Less name-recognition** outside Venezuela than Fe y Alegría.

| Organization | What they do | Why not v1 |

| **FUNDANA** | Temporary homes for children 0–7 in crisis | Different lane (crisis intervention, not education). Worth a future third pillar if propagaluz adds "child protection." |

| **AVEC** (Asociación Venezolana de Educación Católica) | Umbrella for Catholic schools in Venezuela | Less branded, less direct than Fe y Alegría (which is a member). |
| **Cesal** (España, Catholic) | Spanish Catholic dev org in Latin America | Less Venezuela-specific. Possible alternative if Fe y Alegría becomes unworkable; not preferred for v1. |
| **IPYS Venezuela / Espacio Público** | Press-freedom watchdogs | Right values, but adults-focused, not children's-education-focused. Possible thought-partner; not a giving destination. |
| **Direct partnership with a specific school in Venezuela** | Bricks-and-mortar relationship | High operational complexity (vetting, audit, oversight, governance, safety, legal). High reputational risk (one school's trouble = propagaluz's trouble). Audit infrastructure for direct international giving is enormous for a v1 foundation. **Revisit at year 3–5** once propagaluz has its own track record. |

## 5. Recommendation

**Two partners, structured as a complementary pair.**

| Pillar | Partner | What we fund |
|---|---|---|
| **Education** | Fe y Alegría (via Entreculturas in Madrid) | Direct support for a specific Venezuelan school, region, or cohort — ideally in Carabobo state to close the loop with the family's home and El Carabobeño's geography. |
| **Voice** | CECODAP | A specific publication, teacher-training module, or children's-rights program. Something propagaluz can name on the website with a clear deliverable. |

**Why two, not one:**
- Makes propagaluz's two-pillar mission (education + voice) *structurally legible* — there is a partner for each pillar, named on the website. The donor sees the architecture.
- Spreads operational and reputational risk across two vetted organizations.
- Combines a religious-rooted org (Fe y Alegría) and a secular advocacy org (CECODAP) — broadens donor reach.
- Different funding textures (schools vs publications/training) creates two distinct stories to tell.

**Why not three or more:**
- Diffuses impact and storytelling.
- Multiplies governance burden (each partnership needs an agreement, reporting cadence, audit).
- v1 should be tight. Add a third pillar (child protection? journalism mentorship?) only once the first two are running smoothly.

**Why not direct school partnerships:**
- See table §4. Revisit at year 3–5.
