# Logo v1 · Critique

Written before any other typographic or color decisions are made. Reviewed against `BRAND.md` (voice: defiant, child-oriented, devoted; no negatives, no slop, restraint over decoration).

## The mark, described

A stacked uppercase wordmark in heavy condensed sans-serif: **PRO / PAGA / LUZ**, three lines, black on white. A pale buttery-yellow trapezoid behind the wordmark — a beam of light — emerges from inside the middle line and opens out to the right, exiting the right edge of the canvas.

Two colors total: black + cream-yellow. Square aspect ratio. White ground.

## Honest verdict

**Keep this mark as the v1 primary.** Iterate on the system around it, not the mark itself. This is meaningfully better than 90% of student-founded foundation logos because of one structural decision: the beam passes *through* the word, not beside it. That makes the metaphor (propaga + luz) **structural, not decorative.** That single move is what earns the mark.

It is roughly 80% there. Below: what's right, what's worth extending.

## What is right

1. **The beam is integrated, not pasted-on.** Propagation is visualized: light *moving through* the word, not light *sitting near* the word. This is the difference between a logo and an icon.
2. **The beam lands on "PAGA," the middle line that contains OPA.** Whether designed or accidental, the light hits Opa. The grandfather is structurally illuminated. This is grace.
3. **Editorial typography, not friendly-charity typography.** Heavy condensed sans-serif uppercase reads as serious, deliberate, newspaper-adjacent. It echoes the family's journalism heritage and avoids the standard non-profit visual trap (rounded soft letterforms, multi-color systems, gradients).
4. **Restraint.** Two colors. No tagline crammed underneath. No mascot. The mark trusts itself.
5. **The cream-yellow is warm, not aggressive.** Reads as light, not as Cheeto-orange "energy." Aligns with the foundation's quiet defiance.
6. **Stacking handles the long word.** Nine letters laid horizontally would be ungainly or require dramatic shrinking. Stacked in three lines, each line lands with equal weight.

## What to extend

These are not problems with the mark itself. They are system gaps that v1 needs filled before the site goes live.

### 1. The beam reads as a flag, not as light.
Currently the beam is a hard-edged geometric trapezoid. Real beams are softer at the far edge — they fade. A gradient that holds full opacity at the source and falls to roughly 40–60% opacity at the far edge would help the shape read as *light* rather than as *yellow shape*. Worth A/B testing.

### 2. The beam direction needs to be intentional.
It opens rightward, which can read two ways: light cast *from* the word outward (the brand emits), or light arriving *at* the word from off-frame (the brand receives). The foundation's posture is the first. Make sure subsequent visual treatments — the beam's behavior on the site, in motion, in lockups — commit to the brand as *source*, not recipient.

### 3. Lockups missing.
The square mark is the primary. We also need:
- **Horizontal lockup** for page headers, business cards, partner rows. (Could be PROPAGALUZ inline, beam emerging from the final Z; or wordmark left + beam right.)
- **One-line variant** for very narrow contexts (footers, social bio, signatures).
- **Reversed / inverted variants** for dark backgrounds.

### 4. No standalone icon yet.
The full wordmark will not read at 16×16 or 32×32 (favicons, social avatars, app icons). We need an icon-only mark. The most ownable element is the beam itself. Possibilities:
- **(a)** The beam alone — yellow trapezoid on dark, or white-on-yellow.
- **(b)** A "P" or "PL" monogram with a beam echo.
- **(c)** Just the triangle/wedge as an abstract symbol.

Strong vote for **(a) the beam alone**. It is the most ownable graphic move in the mark. It can stand on its own as the foundation's symbol.

### 5. Typeface identity unknown.
The letterforms look like a heavy condensed sans — possibly **Anton**, possibly **Druk Wide Bold** (Commercial Type, paid), possibly a custom drawing. Two paths:
- **License a typeface** (Druk if budget; Anton if free, via Google Fonts). Use it consistently across the brand.
- **Customize / commission a redrawn version** propagaluz owns. More expensive but creates a defensible mark.

Either is fine for v1. **Identify the current one first** so we know what we're working with.

### 6. The yellow needs a backup.
Pale cream-yellow can wash out on low-brightness screens, in certain print conditions, and can read as "off-white" in some lights. Define a *more saturated* secondary yellow for fragile contexts (small sizes, low-contrast environments). Both versions are part of the system — the pale is primary; the saturated is the fallback.

### 7. No dark-mode behavior defined.
What does this mark look like on a dark background? Black-text-on-yellow with the beam in white? Yellow-text-on-black with the beam in yellow? Pick one, write it into DESIGN.md as the rule.

### 8. The OPA is not marked, and that is right.
The middle line "PAGA" contains the foundation's most private detail (Opa, the grandfather, hidden in the name). BRAND.md says: "this is a private intimacy, not a marketing point." The logo respects this — it does not highlight OPA with color, weight, or any other treatment. **Keep it that way.** The intimacy survives because it's not advertised.

## What the visual system needs next (after this mark)

Once the v1 lockup system is in place, the rest of the visual identity follows:

- **Primary palette**: black + the pale cream-yellow already in the mark.
- **Secondary palette**: 1–2 additional warm neutrals + 1 accent. (Probably a warm off-white for backgrounds, a deep red or oxblood for accents tied to El Carabobeño's heritage.)
- **Type system**: a body serif for editorial copy (manifesto, About page) + the wordmark's heavy sans for display. The serif is what earns the journalism legacy in body text.
- **Motion vocabulary**: the beam itself is the motion primitive. Light sweeps across the page. Light fills a card. Light reveals a story. Never decorative; always meaning-bearing.
- **Photography direction**: real, never stock (per BRAND.md).
