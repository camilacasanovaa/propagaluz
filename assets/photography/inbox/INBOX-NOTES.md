## What landed (5 images, 2026-06-06)

### 1. `Eduardo-Aleman.jpg` — Eduardo at his desk reading
Eduardo (Opa) in his director's office, reading what looks like *El Carabobeño*. Recent (probably 2010s). White hair, glasses, white beard. Behind him: framed family photos on the wall, books, the warmth of a long-lived workspace.

- **Mood:** intimate, present, devoted. The man at the work he loved.
- **Public use:** ✅ Strong. The single best portrait of Opa we have. Probably the **closing image** of the `/origen` page, next to *"El medio cambia. El trabajo sigue."*
- **Caveats:** none.

### 2. `Eduardo-Aleman-en-la-rotativa.jpg` — Eduardo at the rotary press
Color photo, Eduardo middle-aged (probably 1990s), in a dark suit, standing next to a Rockwell rotary press. The orange of the machine dominates the frame; he is the human scale next to it. The press is the medium; he is the trade.

- **Mood:** working portrait, dignified, the foundation of the family vocation made literal.
- **Public use:** ✅ Very strong. **Strong candidate for the homepage hero** — speaks the family thread immediately without any caption.
- **Caveats:** none. The Rockwell branding is incidental and historical.

### 3. `Eduardo-Aleman-Eladio-Aleman-y-el-cronista-de-Valencia-Alfonso-Marin-…jpg` — three generations at the press
Black-and-white, three men in suits in a printing facility. Per the filename: Eduardo Alemán + Eladio Alemán + Alfonso Marín (chronicler of Valencia). This is **both grandfathers in the same frame** — Eladio in the center with glasses (the elder), Eduardo on the left with the dark beard (the son). Alfonso Marín on the right.

- **Mood:** historic, multi-generational, the lineage made visible.
- **Public use:** ✅ Strong. **Strong candidate for the `/origen` page hero** or for the multi-generation moment in the home origin teaser. The photograph carries Eladio and Eduardo side by side — the manifesto's "casi cien años" rendered as one image.
- **Caveats:** none. Alfonso Marín is a respected Valencian chronicler; his presence enriches rather than complicates.

### 4. `Inauguracion-de-El-Carabobeno.jpg` — inauguration of the 1997 building
Inauguration of the Naguanagua headquarters (1997, per research). Older man cutting the ribbon (likely **President Rafael Caldera** — confirmed in the legacy research; he supervised the inauguration). Family members and dignitaries flanking. The seated elderly woman on the right may be a family matriarch.

- **Mood:** institutional, ceremonial, civic.
- **Public use:** ⚠️ **Hold for now.** This photograph almost certainly features Rafael Caldera, a Venezuelan president. Even though Caldera is a historical figure (died 2009) and the foundation's rule is *don't name regimes/leaders/parties in copy*, surfacing a president's image on the site, however incidentally, invites a reading we don't need to invite. Two options:
  - **Crop tightly** to family members only and use the cropped version on `/origen` as documentation of the 1997 moment. Caption: *"Inauguración de la sede de El Carabobeño en Naguanagua, 1997."*
  - **Skip for the public site.** Keep as internal archive.
- **Recommendation:** crop, then decide.

### 5. `Contra-el-cierre-de-El-Carabobeno-1.jpg` — Eduardo speaking at a 2016 protest
Color photograph, outdoors at night. Eduardo at a microphone in a casual jacket, speaking. Behind him, three people hold a banner reading **"ATAQUE FULMINANTE CONTRA EL CARABOBEÑO"** ("Sudden attack against El Carabobeño"). This is from the 2015–2016 newsprint denial period, when the family fought to keep the paper in print.

- **Mood:** the defining battle of Opa's final years. Public, defiant, alive.
- **Public use:** ⚠️ **Sensitive.** The image is unmistakably a protest. The banner names an attack on the paper. Per the brand rule (no politics named), this photograph **does name politics** by its visual content even if we add no caption. Two reads:
  - **For:** this image documents a real moment in Opa's life, in his own voice. It honors what he actually did. Putting it on `/origen` is biographical accuracy, not a political endorsement.
  - **Against:** the site is meant to be about the children we serve today, not about media battles. The image risks miscoding the foundation's posture and making it look like it's fighting a political fight that the manifesto explicitly stays out of.
- **Recommendation:** **hold for v1.** Keep as internal archive. Revisit if/when there's a "the work continues" longer-history page where this photo earns its place with proper context.
