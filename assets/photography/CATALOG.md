# Photography · Catalog

What's where, what it shows, what's public.

## Folder structure

- **`family/`** — curated archival photographs cleared for public site use.
- **`camila/`** — portraits of the founder (pending).
- **`letter/`** — scanned/photographed handwritten material (the founder's letter, when ready).
- **`archive/`** — internal-only photographs not used on the public v1 site. Historical record.
- **`inbox/`** — temporary dump location for new images. Cleared after curation.
- **`refs/`** — visual references (charity:water, Max Strong screenshots).

## `family/` · cleared for v1 public use

### `Eduardo-Aleman-Perez-desk.jpg`
Eduardo (Opa) reading the paper at his director's desk. Recent — probably 2010s. White hair, glasses, white beard. Family photographs framed on the wall behind him. Intimate, present, the man at the work he loved.
- **Use:** closing image of `/origen`, beside *"El medio cambia. El trabajo sigue."*

### `Eduardo-Aleman-Perez-rotativa.jpg`
Eduardo middle-aged (probably 1990s), dark suit, standing beside a Rockwell rotary press. The orange of the press dominates; he is the human scale next to it. Working portrait. The medium and the man together.
- **Use:** **homepage hero** candidate. Speaks the family vocation in a single image, no caption needed.

### `Eladio-Eduardo-AlfonsoMarin-tres-generaciones.jpg`
Black-and-white, three men in suits inside an El Carabobeño printing facility. Eduardo on the left (younger, dark beard), Eladio in the center (elder, glasses), Alfonso Marín on the right (Valencia chronicler). Both grandfathers in one frame.
- **Use:** `/origen` hero, or the origin teaser on the homepage. Eladio and Eduardo side by side render the manifesto's *"casi cien años"* as one image.

## `archive/` · internal only, not for v1 public use

### `Inauguracion-El-Carabobeno-1997.jpg`
Inauguration of the Naguanagua headquarters (1997). The man cutting the ribbon is almost certainly **President Rafael Caldera** (per the legacy research — he supervised the inauguration). Family members and dignitaries flank.
- **Decision (2026-06-06):** **hold from v1.** Per the brand rule (no politics named, no leaders surfaced), even an incidental presidential image invites a reading we don't need. If we ever want to use the photo on a longer-history page, a careful crop to family-only is possible — but not for v1.

### `Eduardo-protesta-El-Carabobeno-2016.jpg`
Eduardo at a microphone, outdoors at night, speaking publicly during the 2015–2016 newsprint denial period. Three people behind him hold a banner reading *"ATAQUE FULMINANTE CONTRA EL CARABOBEÑO"* — *Sudden attack against El Carabobeño.*
- **Decision (2026-06-06):** **hold from v1.** The image documents a real moment in Opa's life, but it puts a media battle at the visual center of a site that's meant to be about the children. Brand rule (no politics named in copy) applies to visual content too. Keep as internal archive; revisit if a longer-history page is built later where this earns its place with proper context.
