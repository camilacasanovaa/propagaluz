Propagaluz.

Everything you need to render the propagaluz brand correctly. Logos, colors, type, voice. Pulled directly from the live site.

What is Propagaluz? Propagaluz is a foundation that supports the education of Venezuelan children. It exists to carry resources from outside Venezuela to Fe y Alegría — the country’s largest independent network of schools — and help them stay open. Every dollar that comes in travels to a single place, and stays there.

Tagline. propaga la luz. — spread the light. Use this Spanish tagline as the brand’s signature line. Keep it lowercase, with the period.

Voice

Propagaluz speaks quietly, declaratively, and never politically. Restraint is the brand.

Do

  • Write in short, declarative sentences. Period-driven, not comma-driven.
  • Use Spanish only as accent (the headline, the tagline, Eladio’s line). Body copy is English.
  • Talk about the work, the children, and the partner.
  • Keep all numbers cited (UNICEF, Fe y Alegría, etc.).
  • Speak in positives (“they stay open” vs. “they don’t close”).

Don’t

  • Mention politics, the Venezuelan government, or any specific officials.
  • Use savior language (“saving children,” “rescuing,” “changing lives”).
  • Use family names of the founder or her grandfather in public copy.
  • Write in negatives (“no,” “never,” “not”) when a positive form exists.
  • Add new colors, fonts, or visual treatments without asking.

Photography

When propagaluz uses photography, it follows three rules.

Style

  • Black & white, warm grain, soft contrast.
  • Subject is always children — ideally Venezuelan, ideally in the moment of laughter, learning, or quiet.
  • Avoid posed studio portraits. Lean into editorial / documentary feel.

Usage rule

  • Stock photography from Unsplash or Pexels is permitted (commercial licenses cover us).
  • Never use Fe y Alegría’s own program photography without written permission.
  • Never use a photograph of an identifiable child whose face is centered, unless we own that image.
