# Propagaluz · Origin

## The name

Propagaluz first appeared on a printing press in Valencia, Venezuela, in the early 1930s. It was a small weekly newspaper, written and edited by a young journalist named Eladio Alemán Sucre. The name was an instruction: spread light.

Almost a century later, the same family carries the same name forward, now as a foundation. The same instruction, a different medium.

There is a quieter detail inside the name. At its center, three letters wide, sits **Opa**: the Dutch word for grandfather, what the family called Eduardo Alemán Pérez, the founder's maternal grandfather. Without him, this foundation does not exist.

## Eladio Alemán Sucre · 1906–1984

Camila Casanova's great-grandfather. Born in Ocumare del Tuy, in Venezuela's Miranda state. He learned the trade at a state printing press where *El Mirandino* was published. At nineteen he moved to Valencia, in the state of Carabobo, and opened a bookstore. From there came **El Bolígrafo**, a biweekly. Soon after, El Bolígrafo became **Propagaluz**, a weekly. And on September 1, 1933, in the same city, Eladio founded **El Carabobeño**: a daily that would live for more than ninety years.

He was jailed for twenty-five days and sent into exile in Havana between 1934 and 1936, after a censor misread a story about a locust plague. When he returned, he took the paper back. He was the first president of the Carabobo Journalists Association. In 1941 he served as private secretary to the writer José Rafael Pocaterra during Pocaterra's term as president of Carabobo state. He died in Valencia on June 9, 1984.

His daughter **Marisol Alemán de López**, an architect, designed the paper's new headquarters in Naguanagua in 1997, which also houses the museum of Venezuelan journalism.

What Eladio published over five decades is the philosophical seed of this foundation: the idea that a word in its proper place sets something alight, and that the something can travel.

## Eduardo Alemán Pérez · Opa · 1945–2021

Camila's maternal grandfather. Eladio's son. He studied at Universidad Católica Andrés Bello in Caracas, then at Georgetown and the University of Kansas. He worked on the floor of the *Buffalo Evening News* in New York through 1969. He returned to Venezuela in 1970, and two years later joined El Carabobeño as sub-director. When Eladio died, in 1984, Eduardo took over.

He ran the paper for thirty-seven years. From 1992 he served as president of the Bloque de Prensa Venezolano. From 1994, as a director of the Inter American Press Association, a continent-wide body that defends the freedom of the press. He sustained the trade through years in which sustaining a newspaper cost more and more. When the paper finally ran out, in March 2016, he stopped the daily press after eighty-three years and moved the work to the digital edition, where it continues.

He married **Diana Van der Meer**, of Dutch heritage. Her family's word for grandfather is *opa*, and it stuck. They had four children: Diana Andrea, Eduardo Enrique, Daniel Alberto, and Fabiana. Diana Andrea is Camila's mother.

Eduardo died on November 8, 2021 in Aventura, north of Miami, at seventy-six. Diana was beside him.

## Today

This foundation is the next page. It was founded by **Camila Casanova**, Eduardo's granddaughter, Eladio's great-granddaughter. Born in Miami, raised in Madrid, Venezuelan in what matters most. Her father, **Andrés Casanova Vegas**, holds the foundation legally until she reaches majority, and walks beside her in every step.

Propagaluz today is a bridge. It gathers resources outside Venezuela and hands them to the people already doing the work inside. The intention is small and precise: that a Venezuelan child arrives whole at adulthood, that the count of every euro is clear, that the family's trade continues.

The medium changes. The work remains.
